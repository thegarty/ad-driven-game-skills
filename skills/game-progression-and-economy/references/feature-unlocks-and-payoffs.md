# Feature unlocks and satisfying payoffs

Open complexity when the player has a reason and enough information to use it.
An unlock is a new decision, not merely another icon. The aim is a satisfying
earned outcome and understandable progress; do not describe a design as causing a
specific dopamine response or a proven retention lift without appropriate evidence.

## Build a dependency graph

For each feature, record prerequisite knowledge, input demand, resource access,
first useful situation and how readiness can be observed. Separate a hard rule
from a design assumption. “The player can place a tower” may be a hard dependency
for tower upgrades; “the player must wait until level five” needs a reason.

Use teach → perform → see consequence → try independently. One coached success is
not proof of mastery. Prefer a low-cost practice opportunity and a recoverable
mistake to another paragraph. If the player cannot perform the action, diagnose
input/access problems before withholding more features. Keep optional help
replayable and let experienced players skip teaching where doing so preserves a
safe understandable state.

Never gate essential access controls, audio/motion options, privacy controls,
save/recovery or exit behind mastery, spending or an ad. Keep a viable ordinary
progression route available without ads or purchases.
If a challenge needs a counter, make that counter available and teachable before
requiring it. A gate must have an explainable condition and an attainable route.

## Synthetic sequence

This fictional portrait defence prototype uses short waves. Time windows below
are **illustrative planning assumptions**, not recommended onboarding targets.
Actual pace depends on reading, access needs, retries, interruptions and player
choice. Readiness conditions control availability; elapsed time alone does not.

| Planning window | Decision/feature | Why now; readiness | Earned payoff | Observation and rollback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First session, roughly first minute | Place one basic defender; start a short wave | Player has seen the path and an available placement; a wrong placement can be moved | Immediate placement response, visible hit/defeat and a completed small challenge | Can the player connect placement to protection? Remove competing controls if missed; do not add stronger enemies |
| First session, roughly minutes 1–3 | Spend earned run currency on one upgrade | A successful interaction has produced enough currency; preview makes the change visible | Faster or clearer success against a comparable threat | Can the player explain what improved? Rework cue/preview before changing prices |
| First session, roughly minutes 3–6 | Choose between two counters | Each counter can be demonstrated, and the upcoming threat is readable | A choice changes the outcome; both options have a useful context | Test uninformed selection and a recoverable retry; defer the second choice if it hides the basic rule |
| First session end, whenever reached | One persistent unlock and next-goal recap | Outcome is durable; base earnings already belong to the player | Visible banked progress and one attainable next option | Player can name what persists and next action; simplify recap if the bonus offer obscures earnings |
| Later session after independent counter use | A second route/build slot | Player has independently recognised a threat and used a response, or explicitly opts into the advanced route | New combinations with existing content | If many cannot identify route pressure, provide practice/help; preserve previously granted access |
| Later progression after a full build cycle | Prestige preview, only if this loop needs it | Player has seen a meaningful cap/alternative; preview explains losses and retained benefits | A new strategic route with a known tradeoff | Test prediction of reset result and return journey; disable new resets on a rule defect while preserving earned prestige |

The sequence is a hypothesis. A puzzle may teach one relationship and offer a
new board; an idle game may reveal a processing bottleneck; a roguelite may
introduce a second upgrade option after the first selection has had a visible
effect. Preserve the structure of learning and consequence, not these timings.

## Design the first payoff

Make the outcome attributable to the player's action: a threat stopped, a pattern
solved, a machine unblocked or an upgrade changing play. Distinguish immediate
acknowledgement, demonstrated competence and persistent progress. A shower of
effects without an understandable consequence can obscure all three.

Show already-earned rewards without requiring an ad to reveal or bank them.
An optional offer needs a separate promise and decline route. Do not insert a
full-screen interruption before the player can understand a first success merely
because it is a convenient transition. Measure comprehension and voluntary next
choice alongside the timing of the first meaningful outcome.

## State, migration and returning players

Use stable feature IDs and durable unlock facts. Separate available, introduced,
practised and dismissed-help states; opening a modal is not mastery. Avoid a
rigid timer that unlocks content while the app is backgrounded or while the
player is reading an access setting. Define how offline play and old saves earn
equivalent access when a rule changes.

When sequencing is revised, keep acquired content and earned entitlements.
Newly introduced features can use an optional recap at the next relevant moment.
A returning player may remember the basics but need a short refresher on the
current objective; an expert may need no teaching. Replaying help must not regrant
one-time resources or reset progress.

Use [the unlock schedule](../assets/unlock-schedule.md) to make conditions,
content dependencies, fallback, migration and test ownership reviewable. A failed
rollout can hide a new invitation or disable unsafe new entry while keeping
already-earned access; do not silently relock paid or earned content.

## Validate the sequence

Observe fresh novices, relevant experts and returning players separately. Track
eligible → feature available → introduction encountered → action attempted →
useful consequence → independent use. Record assistance, blocked states,
declined/skipped instruction and missing measurements rather than marking every
introduction view as success.

Change one major dependency or teaching hypothesis at a time when practical.
Check time to first understood success, meaningful choices, voluntary continuation,
frustration/recovery and later cohort return. Faster access may expose complexity
too early; slower access may hide the game's appeal. Neither a shorter tutorial
nor a celebratory animation proves improved retention.
