# Quest design and a worked chain

These are original patterns and **synthetic design examples**, not evidence of a
measured engagement or revenue improvement.

## Select the purpose

| Type | Useful purpose | Common failure | Better acceptance question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onboarding | Learn one action and understand its effect | Completion fires because a menu opened, without understanding | Can the player repeat the useful action independently? |
| Main | Give the next attainable milestone in a longer journey | Several hidden prerequisites or arbitrary level gate | Can the player state the goal and available route? |
| Side | Encourage a different viable strategy or expression | Forced weak loadout, inaccessible input or wasteful spending | Does the optional task create an interesting choice? |
| Daily/weekly | Offer bounded fresh goals suited to natural play | Attendance chores or loss of permanent progress on a missed day | Does the cadence add useful decisions for this audience? |
| Event | Explore temporary content/rules with clear deadlines | Unfinishable assignment near close, ambiguous late claims | Can eligible players reasonably complete and claim under the stated rules? |

Do not assign the same action merely because it is easy to count. “Open the shop
five times” generally measures obedience to a prompt, not useful gameplay.
“Try a slowing defender against a fast wave” can teach a relationship if both are
available and the consequence is visible. Optional exploration should not make
the player's preferred build unusable or consume a scarce resource without a
clear consequence.

## Synthetic first-to-later quest chain

Fictional portrait defence game; no prescribed elapsed times or player results.
Each stage unlocks only after its prerequisites are available and understandable.

| Quest | Readiness | Objective meaning | Payoff and next choice | Failure/recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protect the first seedling | Basic defender, placement and recoverable wave available | Complete the introductory wave with a valid placed defender | Show defence outcome and earned coins | Move/retry/help; settings remain accessible |
| Improve one defence | Enough ordinarily earned coins; preview explains upgrade | Apply one eligible upgrade to a defender; count accepted economy action | Visible change in a comparable wave | No debit on failed action; re-open help without reward repetition |
| Slow a fast threat | Slow defender and fast-enemy cue introduced | In one practice run, a slow effect is applied and the marked wave is completed | Understand a counter and choose a next build | Retry with a contextual clue; no paid/ad-only escape |
| Choose another route | Independent counter use or explicit advanced-route choice | Complete either route A or B; alternatives displayed | One persistent unlock; choose which route to explore next | Preserve earned unlock if quest sequence changes |
| Optional build experiment | Both build families viable and available | Complete a suitable run using a different eligible build family | Cosmetic or bounded progress reward with stated value | Skip without losing main progress; no forced purchase |

Avoid granting several identical “first action” rewards when revising the chain.
Use a migration map from old facts to new objectives. New help can appear when
relevant without resetting completed teaching or awarding again.

## Feasible assignment and generation

Generate only from verified available content, compatible build/region, relevant
player stage and supported input. Check the quest can complete within its active
window under the declared rules, with enough resource capacity to receive the
reward. Distinguish a deterministic impossible task from a challenging task;
likelihood and expected effort require actual assumptions and testing.

Synthetic recurring pool: choose a route challenge only when both routes are
unlocked; otherwise offer a practice-wave objective. Never require an enemy that
has been retired from current content. A seed plus catalog revision makes an
assignment reproducible; validate generated combinations, bound retries and keep
a known valid fallback. Do not charge reroll currency before a replacement exists.

Define reroll eligibility, cost/refund rules, retained progress and reward
comparability. If completing one quest makes another impossible, explain the
choice or prevent the incompatible assignment. A timer should not count down
while the content required to complete it has not been delivered.

## Reward and presentation

Show the objective, progress meaning, reward and relevant deadline where the
player can act. Prefer contextual progress feedback over interrupting play for
every counter increment. Provide a concise next action, with details available
when needed. A fill bar should represent the real target, not a misleading
animation stretched to look nearly complete.

Value the reward against the player's stage and ordinary earning. Claiming should
not hide a mandatory ad or purchase. If an optional bonus exists, keep the earned
quest reward distinct and claimable without it. If inventory can be full, define
a durable mailbox/overflow or delayed claim policy before granting the reward;
do not discard earned content behind an error toast.

## Evaluate the goal

Observe whether players understand the goal, make a useful new choice and want to
continue. Track unavailable assignments, early abandonment, repeated failures,
rerolls, expiry and claim errors. A high completion rate could mean the tasks are
trivial, compulsory or already satisfied; it does not establish improved
engagement. Use comparable mature cohorts and a bounded experiment for broader
retention claims.
